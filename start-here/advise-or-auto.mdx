Authority is a per-environment setting — the **Mode** toggle in the environment's **Autonomy** section (also on the environment panel at the top of **Changes → Remediations**). It is never account-wide, and it changes what may be _authorized_; it never changes what may be _executed_ without a [Permit](/govern/permit). An environment with execution not enabled ([step 9](/start-here/enable-remediation)) is advisory in practice whatever its mode.

## Advise (the default)

*ChangeGuard proposes fixes and waits for a human to approve each one. An approval authorizes exactly that change, once.*

When an incident is detected, ChangeGuard AI proposes a fix — the exact patch, with the reasoning — and it appears in Remediations as **Awaiting approval** with **Approve** and **Reject**. Approving requires an operator, admin or owner; an API key cannot approve. The approval binds the exact action, ChangeGuard issues a Permit, the Executor applies the patch, and ChangeGuard verifies recovery against fresh evidence. The record then reads **Verified**, with its ladder — _Permit issued → Execution started → Action applied → Verifying → Verified_ — and _proposed by changeguard-agent · approved by ‹your email›_. Rejecting keeps the record too, with the reason.

## Auto (opt-in)

ChangeGuard AI may authorize its own proposal — but only inside an explicit **execution policy** for this environment, and never outside it. The policy sits beside the Mode toggle:

| Field | What it bounds |
| --- | --- |
| **Namespaces** | Where Auto may authorize at all. |
| **Fix types** | Which kinds of fix (manifest patches only; code fixes are never auto-executed). |
| **Min confidence** | The floor below which a fix is only proposed, never authorized. |
| **Max / hour** | A cap on automatic authorizations. |

The product refuses to act on an incomplete policy: _Nothing auto-executes — Auto requires a complete policy (namespaces, fix types, confidence floor, hourly cap)._ A fix authorized under Auto records _approved by agent-policy_ together with the policy id and version, and still becomes a single-use Permit consumed by the Executor. Anything outside the policy waits for a person, exactly as in Advise. Editing the policy bumps its version and revokes any Permit issued under the old one.

## Either way

- Every applied fix is **verified** against fresh evidence before it is called successful; a fix that fails verification is handed to a human, and a compensating action needs its own authority and its own Permit.
- Every record keeps **mode at execution**, **approver or policy version**, and **the Permit**, permanently.
- The watching side stays read-only; only the Executor you enabled can write, only where you scoped it, only with a Permit.
