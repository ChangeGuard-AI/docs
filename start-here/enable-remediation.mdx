Everything so far has been read-only. Execution is the one place ChangeGuard AI can _act_ in an environment, and it is built so that the watching side stays read-only no matter what: acting uses a separate identity, registered per environment, with the minimum verbs, scoped to the namespaces you name — and nothing executes without a [Permit](/govern/permit).

Open the environment in **Environments → Connected environments** and scroll to **Execution**. The panel explains the model in one line: _ChangeGuard acts only with a permit: one approved change, to one workload, valid for a few minutes, used once. Enabling execution approves nothing._

## What happens without execution enabled

ChangeGuard AI still detects incidents (**Changes → Incidents**: every failing workload, attributed to the change that shipped it), explains them, and proposes a fix with the exact patch. It just cannot apply anything. Many teams run this way for a while.

## Enabling the Cloud Executor (preferred)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the namespaces">
    Click **Set up Cloud Executor** and name the namespaces ChangeGuard may act in — for example `shop, payments`. `kube-*` namespaces are never granted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the execution role in your AWS account">
    ChangeGuard generates an IAM role for **this environment** — `ChangeGuardExecutor-<environment-instance>` — with **no AWS permissions** and a trust policy that admits only ChangeGuard's Cloud Executor with your external ID. CloudFormation, Terraform and CLI forms are provided; nothing is typed by hand.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the role reach this cluster, and grant the namespaces">
    One generated EKS access entry maps the role to this environment's own Kubernetes group, and one generated RBAC file grants that group `get`, `list` and `patch` on Deployments, StatefulSets and DaemonSets in exactly the namespaces you chose. You apply both with your own credentials — applying them _is_ the grant. Nothing in ChangeGuard AI can give itself write access.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify execution access">
    Click **Verify execution access**. Within about a minute the panel reads **Cloud Executor Ready** and lists every check: ChangeGuard's own (signing, the execution role, the cluster identity, the Executor reachable) and the Executor's (assume the role, authenticate to exactly this cluster, hold the required verbs in your namespaces, hold **none** of the forbidden ones, verify a signed probe Permit). No workload is read or changed by this check.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Prefer an Executor inside your cluster? **Install Local Executor** generates a complete, environment-specific manifest instead; apply it and verify the same way. See [Local Executor](/execute/local-executor). Installing ChangeGuard Edge is not required for either.

## What the Executor may do

- Act **only with a consumed Permit** — one approved change, one workload, minutes, once. Enabling execution approves nothing.
- Only `get`, `list` and `patch` on the workloads in the namespaces you chose. No cluster-admin, no wildcards, no secrets, no `delete`, no `create`, no `pods/exec`.
- Apply only fixes ChangeGuard AI proposed _and_ that were approved by a person (Advise) or allowed by your policy (Auto) — [step 10](/start-here/advise-or-auto).
- Every applied fix is **verified** against fresh evidence before it is called successful, and every record keeps its approver, its policy version and its Permit.

Deleting the access entry, the RoleBinding or the role revokes the grant immediately; the evidence side is unaffected. You can also revoke any live Permit, or disable execution for the environment, from the same panel.
