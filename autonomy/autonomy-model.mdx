ChangeGuard is **autonomous-capable by design and human-controlled by default**. How much authority it has is decided per environment, never account-wide, and in one of two modes:

| Mode | What ChangeGuard may do | What must exist before anything executes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advise** (default) | Detect, diagnose, propose the exact fix, prepare it for execution, and — once authorized — execute and verify it | A **human authorization**: a signed-in operator, admin or owner approves this exact change. API keys cannot approve. |
| **Auto** (opt-in) | Everything in Advise, plus authorize its own proposal **inside an explicit policy** | A complete, non-empty **execution policy** for this environment — namespaces, fix types, a confidence floor, an hourly cap — with a policy id and version. Outside the policy, the change waits for a human exactly as in Advise. |

Either way, authority becomes a [ChangeGuard Permit](/govern/permit) bound to the exact action, and an [Executor](/execute/cloud-executor) carries out only the permitted action. Judgment never authorizes; a verdict of SHIP is a belief, not a grant.

<Note>
  **Three things, kept apart.** _Evidence_ is what ChangeGuard knows about an environment (Fleet, Edge) — read-only. The _mode_ is how much authority you gave it in that environment. The _Executor_ is whether it can act at all — a separately enabled, separately identified path proven by Verify execution access. An environment with no Executor is advisory in practice whatever its mode.
</Note>

## Where you set it

Open the environment in **Connected environments** (or the environment panel at the top of **Changes → Remediations**). The **Mode** toggle reads **Advise** or **Auto**; the policy editor sits beside it. An environment with no explicit setting is Advise.

## Advise — the default

When ChangeGuard proposes a fix, the change appears as **Awaiting approval** with **Approve** and **Reject**. Approving records your identity and binds the canonical hash of the exact action; ChangeGuard then issues a Permit and the Executor applies the fix. The record keeps _proposed by changeguard-agent · approved by ‹you›_ permanently, together with the Permit and its outcome. Rejecting keeps the record too, with the reason.

A fix that is applied is verified against fresh evidence before it is called successful; a fix that fails verification is handed back to a human. Nothing in Advise executes on ChangeGuard's say-so.

## Auto — bounded by policy, falling back to Advise

In Auto, ChangeGuard may authorize its own proposal — but only when **all** of the following hold:

1. the environment's mode is Auto and its policy is complete: namespaces, fix types, a confidence floor and an hourly cap, each non-empty;
2. the proposal is inside every dimension — the target namespace is allowed, the fix type is allowed (manifest patches only; code fixes are never auto-executed), the analysis confidence meets the floor, and the hourly cap has headroom;
3. the action passes the same server-side allowlist as every other change.

Anything outside the policy is created as **Awaiting approval** for a human, exactly as in Advise. An empty or default policy **fails closed**: nothing auto-executes, and the product says so. A fix authorized by policy records _approved by agent-policy_ and the policy id and version that authorized it, so history always says who decided.

The policy is re-derived at the moment of execution, not just at proposal time: editing a policy bumps its version and revokes every Permit issued under the old one. A change already consumed completes under the authority it had; a change not yet consumed returns to a human. It is never re-authorized silently under the new version.

## Either way

- Every applied fix is **verified** against fresh evidence of the environment before it is called successful. No observation, no VERIFIED.
- A failed fix is handed to a human. A compensating action needs its own authority and its own Permit.
- Every record keeps the **mode at execution**, the **approver or policy version**, and the **Permit** — permanently.
- Nothing in the loop can move the mode, widen the policy or approve on your behalf. Lowering authority is always safe: it can only reduce what happens next.
