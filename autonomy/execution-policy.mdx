The execution policy is the contract that bounds what **Auto** may authorize in one environment. It is deny-by-default in every dimension: a new environment has no policy, and an empty or incomplete policy means **nothing auto-executes**, even with the mode set to Auto. Everything outside the policy waits for a human, exactly as in Advise.

You edit it per environment, beside the **Mode** toggle, in **Connected environments** or the environment panel on **Changes → Remediations**. The editor always shows a plain-language summary of the net effect, for example:

> Auto may apply manifest patches in _production, payments_ at _high_\+ confidence, up to _5_/hour.

or, when the policy is incomplete:

> Nothing auto-executes — Auto requires a complete policy (namespaces, fix types, confidence floor, hourly cap).

## Policy dimensions

| Dimension | What it controls | Behavior when unset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Namespaces** | An explicit allowlist of namespaces where fixes may be authorized automatically | No namespace allowed — nothing executes |
| **Fix types** | Which remediation classes may be authorized automatically. Today: `manifest_patch`. Code fixes are never auto-executed | No type allowed — nothing executes |
| **Minimum confidence** | The floor on analysis confidence (`low` / `medium` / `high`) a fix must meet | No confidence accepted — nothing executes |
| **Max per hour** | A hard cap on automatic authorizations per hour, per environment | Zero — nothing executes |

## Identity and versioning

Every explicit policy has an **id** and a **version**. A Permit issued under Auto cites both — together with the scope and the rule that matched — so every record can say exactly which policy decided. The tenant-wide fallback that predates per-environment policies has no identity and therefore can never authorize anything automatically.

## When the policy is evaluated

The policy is not a one-time gate. It is enforced when a change is proposed **and re-derived at consume**, the moment the Executor is about to act:

1. **At proposal.** When analysis completes in an Auto environment, ChangeGuard checks every dimension before authorizing the change under `agent-policy` and issuing a Permit. Anything outside the policy becomes **Awaiting approval** for a human instead.
2. **At consume.** Before the Executor may act, ChangeGuard re-checks that the environment is still in Auto under the **same policy id and version**. If not, consume is refused and the Permit is revoked (`policy_changed`).

**Editing a policy** — broader or narrower, or changing the mode — bumps the version and revokes every Permit issued under the old one. A change already consumed is past the commit point and completes under the authority it had. A change not yet consumed returns to a human. Nothing is ever re-authorized silently under the new version: Auto authority is decided when a change is proposed, so a change proposed after the edit is authorized by, and cites, the new version.

## Interaction with hard safety limits

The policy is an envelope _inside_ platform-level guarantees you cannot accidentally disable:

- Every manifest patch is validated server-side against a **field allowlist** of safe operational fields (resource limits and requests, replicas, environment variables, probe tuning, and similar). Patches touching identity or metadata are rejected outright, whatever the policy says.
- The Executor's own identity is namespace-scoped by the RoleBindings you applied, so even a maximally permissive policy cannot reach namespaces you never granted. See [Execution authority and least privilege](/autonomy/rbac-boundaries).
- Only one remediation per workload is in flight at a time; a second apply is refused.
- Every automatic authorization still becomes a single-use, expiring [Permit](/govern/permit), consumed once.

<Warning>
  Treat the namespace allowlist as your primary blast-radius control and start narrow: one non-critical namespace, `high` confidence, a low hourly cap. Widen it only after you have watched the [audit trail](/autonomy/audit-trail) for a while and trust what you see.
</Warning>
