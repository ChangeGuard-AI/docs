This page is one path with no branching, and it ends at the moment the product has to prove itself: the first recommendation you can check against evidence. Follow the eight steps in order — about half an hour — and you will have installed ChangeGuard AI, seen your first score and verdict, triggered a safe sample incident, and read an evidence-cited investigation of it on your own cluster.

<Note>
  Everything here is **advisory and read-only**. Nothing in this guide changes your workloads (the sample incident happens in a scratch namespace you create and delete). ChangeGuard does not act on your cluster until you explicitly opt into [remediation](/autonomy/autonomy-model).
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install ChangeGuard">
    One command installs the operator and the read-only collector — the [install page](/get-started/install) walks it through. Already installed? Carry straight on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect your cluster">
    Confirm the operator and collector are healthy and your cluster appears in **Fleet** with green connection indicators ("Agent connected", "Workloads detected") — the checks are on [Connect your first cluster](/get-started/connect-first-cluster). If anything doesn't match, [the fix table there](/get-started/connect-first-cluster#if-a-step-didnt-produce-the-result-shown) covers it — it's almost always egress or the API key.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See your first score">
    Your cluster's **CSC Score** appears in Fleet within ~10 seconds of connecting — a deterministic 0–100 read of deploy readiness. Every point ties to a concrete signal you can inspect: [Understanding your score](/how-it-works).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run a pre-flight check — and meet the Advisor">
    Open **Safe to Ship?** and run a pre-flight check. You get **SHIP / HOLD / BLOCK** with the score and reasons — advisory, never an enforced block. Where the [Engineering Advisor](/get-started/engineering-advisor) is enabled (Early Access), its note appears alongside the verdict: the read a senior engineer would give.

    <Info>
      Two things that are correct, not broken: if the Advisor has nothing material to add it stays **silent** — restraint is the feature. And if you don't see the Advisor at all, that's expected — it's Early Access, off by default.
    </Info>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trigger a sample incident, safely">
    Watch a failure get correlated to the change that caused it — in a scratch namespace that touches nothing real:

    ```bash
    # 1. A healthy sample service
    kubectl create namespace cg-sample
    kubectl -n cg-sample create deployment sample-api --image=nginx:1.27-alpine --replicas=2
    kubectl -n cg-sample rollout status deployment/sample-api --timeout=120s
    
    # 2. Ship a bad change — a typo'd image tag, the classic bad deploy
    kubectl -n cg-sample set image deployment/sample-api nginx=nginx:1.27-alpine-nonexistent
    ```

    The new pods can't pull the image, the rollout starts failing, and ChangeGuard sees both the change and the failure. Within a few minutes, an incident appears for `sample-api` — **linked to the image change you just made**. Cause, not just symptom.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understand the recommendation">
    Open the incident. The root cause comes with **cited evidence** — the events, the failing rollout, and the change that shipped it. ChangeGuard proposes the fix and states the criteria to verify it worked. At the default autonomy level it applies nothing — this is advice you act on. Where the **Engineering Opinion** is enabled (Early Access), you'll also see an owned position: belief, confidence, tradeoffs, and what would change its mind.
  </Step>
  <Step title="View the Current Understanding">
    Where incident context is enabled, the incident opens with the **Current Understanding** — the maintained read of what's happening right now: what changed, what it broke, and where things stand. It's what a teammate joining mid-incident needs, without scrolling the history. Every incident also carries an append-only **activity timeline** — your audit trail of what was observed and decided.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See the loop close">
    Ship the fix and watch ChangeGuard observe the recovery:

    ```bash
    # 3. Revert — the "rollback" a real team would do
    kubectl -n cg-sample set image deployment/sample-api nginx=nginx:1.27-alpine
    
    # 4. When you're done exploring, remove every trace
    kubectl delete namespace cg-sample
    ```

    The rollout recovers, the incident reflects it, and the timeline records the whole arc — change, failure, fix, recovery. You applied this fix yourself; if you later opt into [Autonomous Remediation](/autonomy/autonomy-model), ChangeGuard can execute fixes within the policy you set, **verify** them against real workload health, and roll back once if verification fails.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Set your comfort level

ChangeGuard's autonomy is a dial, not a switch: **Observe → Advise → Approve → Auto**, default **Advise**.

| Level | What ChangeGuard does |
| --- | --- |
| **Observe** | Watches and records only. |
| **Advise** _(default)_ | Recommends actions; a human does everything. |
| **Approve** | Prepares an action and waits for a human to authorize it. |
| **Auto** | Executes within policy, then verifies — and rolls back once if verification fails. |

<Warning>
  `Approve` and `Auto` only do anything after you opt into [Autonomous Remediation](/autonomy/autonomy-model) and grant its RBAC. Until then, ChangeGuard stays at advice.
</Warning>

## You've now seen the loop

In 30 minutes: a verified install, a scored cluster, a pre-flight verdict, a failure correlated to its cause, an evidence-cited investigation, and the recovery on the timeline — plus, where enabled, the Understanding, Opinion, and Advisor that turn it into a teammate.
